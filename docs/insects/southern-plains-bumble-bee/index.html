Southern Plains bumble bee

Bombus fraternus

A large, short-haired bumble bee of open country, which the Fish & Wildlife Service is studying for listing.

Under review, not listed. The Fish & Wildlife Service is studying whether to list this insect. Until it does, the insect has no protection under the Endangered Species Act.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this insect. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A large black and yellow bumble bee with uniformly short hair and a short head. The head and thorax can be largely yellow. Queens are just over an inch long.

Where it lives

Open prairies, meadows, grasslands, and pine savannas. The Xerces Society also finds it in towns.

In Alabama

The Xerces Society counts Alabama among the states in its range. The sources read for this entry say nothing more exact about where in the state it lives, and the Alabama Natural Heritage Program's list of 2021 does not track it.

Why it is at risk

Loss of habitat and the use of pesticides are thought to be the largest causes. Disease, parasites, competition with managed bees, and a changing climate are named as well. The Xerces Society reports that it has grown scarcer among bumble bees over the past decade.

Notes

The Service was petitioned in July 2022 to list it as endangered. On January 25, 2024 it found that listing may be warranted, and opened a review. No proposal had followed when this site was last checked.
