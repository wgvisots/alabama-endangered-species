Monarch

Danaus plexippus

The familiar orange-and-black migrant, proposed for listing as threatened but not yet protected.

Proposed, not listed. This insect has no protection under the Endangered Species Act unless a final rule lists it.

What it looks like

A large butterfly with a wingspan of 3 to 5 inches. The wings are orange with black veins and a wide black border dotted with white. Males are the brighter, and carry a dark scent patch on each hindwing. The caterpillar is banded in white, black, and yellow, with a pair of long black filaments at each end. The chrysalis is green with gold dots.

Where it lives

Sunny, open places of nearly any kind: fields, roadsides, wet ground, coasts, and gardens. It needs milkweed for its caterpillars and flowers for nectar.

How it lives

The caterpillars eat only milkweeds and their close relatives. Several short-lived generations breed through spring and summer. The last generation of the year flies south, as far as 3,000 miles, to winter in high fir forests in central Mexico, and those butterflies can live six to nine months.

In Alabama

Recorded in every county. Most Alabamians see monarchs only in spring and fall, as the migration passes through, and the highest counts have come from Baldwin County in October. Some monarchs spend the winter on the Gulf Coast, roosting in trees at Fort Morgan and near Fairhope.

Why it is at risk

Loss of milkweed across the breeding range, logging at the wintering grounds in Mexico, and a parasite that weakens caterpillars and adults. The Service puts the chance that the eastern migratory population will be extinct by 2080 at 56 to 74 percent.

Notes

The Fish & Wildlife Service proposed listing the monarch as threatened on December 12, 2024. No final rule has followed, so the monarch has no protection under the Act today. Under a court settlement filed on September 30, 2026, the Service is to make its final decision by June 6, 2030. For gardeners, the Alabama Butterfly Atlas recommends native milkweeds over tropical milkweed, which can prolong breeding and spread the parasite.
