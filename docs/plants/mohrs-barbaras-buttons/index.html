Mohr's Barbara's buttons

Marshallia mohrii

A pale pink, button-flowered herb of moist glades and prairie openings.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this plant. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

An upright perennial 1 to 2.5 feet tall. The firm, lance-shaped leaves have three parallel veins and are largest near the base. Each stem carries several flower heads about an inch across, made up entirely of tubular flowers that are white to pale pink or lavender.

Where it grows

Open to partly shaded ground dominated by grasses and sedges: dolomite glades, prairie remnants, stream margins, and woodland openings. Several populations are on roadsides and utility rights-of-way.

In Alabama

The Service's 2016 review found surviving populations in Bibb, Calhoun, Cherokee, Jefferson, and Walker Counties. Older reports also name Blount, Cullman, and Etowah Counties.

Why it is at risk

Conversion of habitat to pine plantations, farms, and building sites; fire exclusion; mowing or herbicide at the wrong time; and invasive plants, chiefly Chinese privet.

Notes

The 2016 review counted 19 surviving populations out of 28 known.
