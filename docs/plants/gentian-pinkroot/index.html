Gentian pinkroot

Spigelia gentianoides

A small pink-flowered herb of fire-kept pinelands, with a close relative on the Bibb County glades.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this plant. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A perennial herb with a slender, reddish-tinged stem up to about 30 cm tall and opposite leaves up to 5 cm long. The pink, tubular flowers are up to 3 cm long, with five pointed lobes that stay nearly closed at the tip.

Where it grows

Well-drained upland pine woods kept open by fire, in longleaf pine and wiregrass country, and mixed pine, oak, and hickory woods.

In Alabama

Known in Alabama from Geneva County. The Service's five-year review counts seven surviving populations and about 3,900 plants in Geneva County and three counties of the Florida Panhandle.

Why it is at risk

Conversion of pinelands to pine plantations, development, and the loss of regular fire, which lets the canopy close and shade the plants out.

Notes

A related plant on the dolomite glades of Bibb County was first treated as a variety of this species (var. alabamensis) and was named a separate species, Spigelia alabamensis, in 2011. The five-year review says the listing now covers only the Florida and Geneva County plants, and calls for the recovery plan to be revised to match.
