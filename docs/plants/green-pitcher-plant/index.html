Green pitcher plant

Sarracenia oreophila

A carnivorous plant of upland bogs and seeps, known from northeast Alabama and a few sites in Georgia and North Carolina.

What it looks like

A carnivorous perennial that grows from a rhizome. In spring it sends up hooded, tube-shaped pitcher leaves 20 to 75 cm tall, green to yellow-green and sometimes veined with maroon, and a single yellow flower on a stalk 45 to 70 cm tall. By late summer the pitchers wither and are replaced by flat, sickle-shaped leaves.

Where it grows

Seepage bogs, sandstone streambanks, and moist oak-pine woodland on poor, acidic soils. Its sites dry out quickly in midsummer.

In Alabama

Reported from Cherokee, DeKalb, Etowah, Jackson, and Marshall Counties in the northeast, and from Elmore County.

Why it is at risk

Drainage and development of its bog habitat, and collecting for the plant trade, which the recovery plan names as the most serious threat. Collecting it is illegal, and international trade in wild plants is barred under CITES Appendix I.

Notes

About 34 natural populations remain, and most hold fewer than 50 plants.
