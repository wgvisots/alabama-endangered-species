Tennessee yellow-eyed grass

Xyris tennesseensis

A clump-forming wetland plant whose small yellow flowers open for only a few hours a day.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this plant. A photo by Al Schotz, Alabama Natural Heritage Program, on the FWS site (copyrighted, shown there by permission).

What it looks like

A perennial that grows in clumps from fleshy, bulb-like bases, 2.3 to 3.3 feet tall. The leaves are long, narrow, twisted, and deep green. Brown, cone-shaped flower heads sit singly on slender stalks, and from each a pale yellow flower about a fifth of an inch across opens in late morning.

Where it grows

Open or lightly shaded seeps, fens, wet meadows, and gravelly stream edges, on thin, near-neutral soils over limestone and similar rock.

In Alabama

The Service lists ten Alabama populations: four each in Bibb and Calhoun Counties, and one each in Franklin and Shelby Counties.

Why it is at risk

Draining and filling of wetlands, road building, quarrying, logging, grazing, and off-road vehicles. Without management, woody plants shade it out. Most sites are privately owned.

Notes

The Service counts 21 surviving populations across the three states.
