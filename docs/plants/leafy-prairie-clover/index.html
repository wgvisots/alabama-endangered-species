Leafy prairie clover

Dalea foliosa

A purple-flowered prairie clover of thin-soiled limestone glades.

What it looks like

A perennial herb 20 to 80 cm tall, with several upright stems from a hard root crown. The leaves are divided into small, hairless, oval leaflets. The flowers are small and purple, packed into dense, cone-shaped spikes at the stem tips.

Where it grows

Thin soils over limestone in cedar glades, barrens, and prairie openings, often where seepage keeps the ground moist.

In Alabama

Reported from Colbert, Franklin, Jefferson, Lawrence, and Morgan Counties. Most known populations are in Tennessee.

Why it is at risk

Loss of glades to development, overgrazing, and road and sewer work. Where fire is kept out, cedars and invasive shrubs such as Chinese privet and bush honeysuckle shade it out.
