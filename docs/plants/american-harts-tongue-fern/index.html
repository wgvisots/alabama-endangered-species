American hart's-tongue fern

Asplenium scolopendrium var. americanum

A strap-leaved evergreen fern that survives in the South only in cool limestone sinkholes.

What it looks like

An evergreen fern with a crown of undivided, strap-shaped fronds, typically 35 to 40 cm long and 2 to 4.5 cm wide, with smooth edges and ear-like lobes at the base. The spore clusters are brown lines set in two rows on the underside of each frond.

Where it grows

Cool, moist, shaded limestone. In the North it grows on talus slopes and boulder fields. In the South it is confined to sinkholes and cave mouths, which stay cooler than the land around them.

In Alabama

Known from limestone sinks in Jackson and Morgan Counties. The 2019 status assessment reports that one Alabama population, on national wildlife refuge land, was gone by 2012, and that Alabama and Tennessee together held only two populations and roughly 30 plants.

Why it is at risk

Logging, development, and quarrying caused most past losses. Invasive plants, drought, a changing climate, collecting, and trampling by visitors are the present concerns.
