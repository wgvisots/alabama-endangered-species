Harperella

Ptilimnium nodosum

A slender, quill-leaved member of the carrot family that grows on rocky stream shoals.

What it looks like

A perennial herb 6 to 36 inches tall. Its leaves are reduced to hollow, quill-like tubes, and its small white flowers are held in flat-topped clusters that resemble Queen Anne's lace.

Where it grows

Gravelly and rocky shoals of clear streams, and the edges of shallow upland ponds. It tolerates only a narrow range of water depths.

In Alabama

Reported from Cherokee, Cullman, DeKalb, Tuscaloosa, and Walker Counties.

Why it is at risk

Changes to stream flow and water quality: dams, diversions, and reservoirs that drown or dry the shoals, and silt from construction, farming, and development.

Notes

Some botanists split harperella into three species and treat the Alabama plants as Ptilimnium fluviatile. The federal listing covers all of them under Ptilimnium nodosum.
