Georgia rockcress

Arabis georgiana

A white-flowered mustard of steep river bluffs.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this plant. A photo by Alan Cressler on the FWS site (copyrighted, shown there by permission).

What it looks like

A perennial herb up to about 90 cm tall. It has a basal rosette of toothed, lance-shaped leaves 4 to 8 cm long, and smaller leaves that partly clasp the stem. The flowers are white with four petals, and the fruit is a slender, upright pod 5 to 7 cm long.

Where it grows

Steep river bluffs with shallow soil over rock, rock outcrops, and sandy, eroding riverbanks, in full to partial sun. It occasionally grows in limestone glades.

In Alabama

Critical habitat was designated in Bibb, Dallas, Elmore, Monroe, Sumter, and Wilcox Counties. The 2021 status assessment also notes a site in Russell County, along the Chattahoochee River.

Why it is at risk

Invasive nonnative plants are the primary threat. Quarrying, development, roads, timber harvest, reservoir flooding, and shade from a closing forest canopy also reduce its habitat.

Notes

Critical habitat for this species was designated on September 12, 2014 (79 FR 54635).
