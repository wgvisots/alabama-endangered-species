Alabama leather flower

Clematis socialis

A low clematis that does not climb, known from a handful of colonies with blue-violet bells.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this plant. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

Unlike most clematis, it does not climb. It spreads by underground stems and forms dense patches of upright shoots up to about 30 cm tall. Each shoot carries one or a few urn-shaped, blue-violet flowers 2 to 3 cm long. The lower leaves are simple and narrow, and the upper leaves are divided into three to five leaflets.

Where it grows

Wet, silty-clay flats near creeks, at the edge of bottomland woods and in grass and sedge openings. Every known site has been altered by people, and several are on road or utility rights-of-way.

In Alabama

First found beside a highway in St. Clair County. It is reported from Cherokee, Etowah, and St. Clair Counties. As of 2010 there were five known populations, four in Alabama and one in Georgia.

Why it is at risk

Road resurfacing, pipeline and utility work, herbicides, and mowing at the wrong season. Erosion, competition from other plants, and seed-eating mice also press on the small colonies.
