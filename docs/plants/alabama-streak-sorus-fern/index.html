Alabama streak-sorus fern

Thelypteris pilosa var. alabamensis

A small fern of shaded sandstone overhangs, known from a single river drainage.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this plant. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A small fern with narrow, once-divided fronds, usually 3.5 to 10 cm long and 1.3 to 3.3 cm wide, dull yellow-green and finely hairy on both sides. The spore clusters are elongated streaks, about 5 mm long, without the protective flap that most ferns have. That streak gives the plant its name.

Where it grows

Shaded, humid sandstone overhangs and rock faces along the river, under forest that keeps the rock cool and moist.

In Alabama

Found only in Alabama, and only along the Sipsey Fork in Winston County, within Bankhead National Forest.

Why it is at risk

Rising water behind the reservoir downstream, loss of forest cover and of hemlocks above the bluffs, development of private land, and heavy recreational use of the river corridor.

Notes

Recent treatments call this fern Leptogramma burksiorum or Thelypteris burksiorum. The federal list still uses Thelypteris pilosa var. alabamensis.
