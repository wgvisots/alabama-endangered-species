Pondberry

Lindera melissifolia

A low, thicket-forming shrub of seasonally flooded woods that flowers before its leaves appear.

What it looks like

A deciduous shrub, usually low but now and then over 6 feet tall, that spreads by runners into colonies. The thin, drooping leaves smell of sassafras when crushed. Small, pale yellow flowers open in late winter before the leaves, with male and female flowers on separate plants. The fruit is a bright red, oval drupe about half an inch long.

Where it grows

Seasonally flooded wetlands: bottomland hardwood forest, and the margins of sinks, ponds, and depressions, usually in light shade.

In Alabama

Reported from Covington and Wilcox Counties.

Why it is at risk

Ditching and drainage, which change how long the ground stays wet, and conversion of wetlands to other uses. Timber harvest, hogs and cattle, and laurel wilt disease are also concerns.
