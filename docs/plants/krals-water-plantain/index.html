Kral's water-plantain

Sagittaria secundifolia

An aquatic arrowhead rooted among the boulders of a few clear, rocky streams.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this plant. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A perennial aquatic herb with a stout rhizome. The leaves take their shape from the water: in fast shallows they are stiff, sickle-shaped, and 5 to 8 cm long, and in quiet pools they are quill-like and 10 to 30 cm long. The flower stalks are 10 to 50 cm tall, with white male flowers 1 to 1.5 cm wide.

Where it grows

Frequently exposed shoals and quiet pools up to about a meter deep, rooted among loose boulders in narrow streams bordered by steep slopes.

In Alabama

Reported from the Little River in Cherokee and DeKalb Counties, Hatchet Creek in Coosa County, and the Sipsey Fork in Winston County. Sites on federal land include Little River Canyon National Preserve and Bankhead National Forest.

Why it is at risk

Silt from logging and erosion, falling water quality and nutrient enrichment from sewage, impoundments, and heavy recreational use of the river corridors.
