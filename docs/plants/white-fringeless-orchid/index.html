White fringeless orchid

Platanthera integrilabia

A white, sweet-scented orchid of shaded seeps and streamhead swamps.

What it looks like

A perennial orchid up to about 2 feet tall, with a loose spike of up to 20 white, fragrant flowers. Each flower has a long, curving nectar spur and a rounded lip without the fringe that marks its relatives.

Where it grows

Wet, flat, boggy ground at the heads of streams and on seepage slopes, in partial shade, often with sphagnum moss.

In Alabama

Reported from Calhoun, Clay, Marion, and Tuscaloosa Counties.

Why it is at risk

Draining of wet sites, road building, careless timber harvest, and herbicides. Deer, feral hogs, invasive plants, shade from a closing canopy, and poaching add to the pressure. Most populations hold fewer than 100 plants.
