Little amphianthus

Amphianthus pusillus

A thumbnail-sized annual that lives its whole life in rain pools on granite outcrops.

What it looks like

A tiny aquatic annual only a few centimeters long. A rosette of small leaves sits underwater, and a thread-like stem carries a pair of floating oval leaves, up to about 8 mm long, to the surface. The flowers are white and only a few millimeters wide. It grows fast and can mature in under three weeks.

Where it grows

Shallow, temporary pools on granite and gneiss outcrops. The pools hold water in late winter and spring and are dry by summer.

In Alabama

Reported from Chambers, Randolph, and Tallapoosa Counties, at the western edge of a range centered in Georgia.

Why it is at risk

Quarrying of granite outcrops is considered the greatest threat. Off-road vehicles, dumping, and nutrient runoff from livestock also foul the pools.

Notes

Many botanists now place this plant in the genus Gratiola, as Gratiola amphiantha. The federal list still uses Amphianthus pusillus.
