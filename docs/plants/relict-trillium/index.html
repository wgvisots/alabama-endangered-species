Relict trillium

Trillium reliquum

A spring wildflower with mottled leaves and a stem that curves along the ground.

What it looks like

A perennial herb with a whorl of three mottled leaves, patterned in greens, bronze, and purple, often with a silver stripe along the midvein. The smooth stem is S-shaped and tends to lie along the ground. A single stalkless flower sits at the center of the leaves, with petals that range from dark purple to greenish or yellow.

Where it grows

Moist, rich soils in undisturbed hardwood forest, often along stream banks and small floodplains.

In Alabama

Reported from Bullock, Henry, and Lee Counties in the southeast.

Why it is at risk

Clearing of hardwood forest for farming and pine plantations. Heavy browsing by white-tailed deer also sets the plants back.
