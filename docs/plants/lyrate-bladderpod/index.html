Lyrate bladderpod

Lesquerella lyrata

A yellow-flowered annual mustard known from just three populations, all in northwest Alabama.

What it looks like

An annual 10 to 30 cm tall, with short hairs on the stems and leaves. The basal leaves are lyre-shaped, with a large rounded end lobe. The stem leaves have no stalks and clasp the stem with small ear-like lobes. The flowers are bright yellow, and the seed pods are smooth and nearly round.

Where it grows

Cedar glades on limestone. The surviving populations grow in glade-like disturbed ground: pastures, roadsides, and cultivated fields.

In Alabama

Found only in Alabama, with one population each in Colbert, Franklin, and Lawrence Counties. The Lawrence County population is the largest and is protected on a Nature Conservancy preserve.

Why it is at risk

Home building near the Colbert and Franklin County sites, and roadside mowing or herbicide before the plants set seed. The seeds can wait in the soil for years, so a site that seems lost may return under good management.

Notes

Many botanists now call this plant Paysonia lyrata. The federal list still uses Lesquerella lyrata.
