Fleshy-fruit gladecress

Leavenworthia crassa

A winter annual of cedar glades that grows nowhere on earth but two counties in north Alabama.

What it looks like

A hairless winter annual 10 to 40 cm tall. The leaves form a flat rosette and are deeply lobed, with the end lobe much the largest. The four-petaled flowers are yellow with orange, or white with yellow, and both forms often grow in the same population. The fruit is a plump, rounded pod about 12 mm long with a slender beak.

Where it grows

Open, sunny cedar glades on thin soil over limestone, wet in winter and spring and baked dry in summer. Most remaining plants are in glade-like pastures, roadsides, and field edges. Seeds sprout in September and October, and the plants pass the winter as rosettes.

In Alabama

Found only in Alabama, in the Moulton and Tennessee Valleys of Lawrence and Morgan Counties. The Service counts ten occurrences in eight populations. All but one are on private land, and one site lies within Bankhead National Forest.

Why it is at risk

Conversion of glades to farmland and building sites, roadside maintenance, and shade and competition as woody and invasive plants move in.
