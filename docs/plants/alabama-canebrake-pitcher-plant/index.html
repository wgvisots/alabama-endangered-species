Alabama canebrake pitcher plant

Sarracenia rubra ssp. alabamensis

A maroon-flowered pitcher plant found only in a few seepage bogs in central Alabama.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this plant. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A carnivorous perennial that grows from a rhizome and makes two sets of pitchers each year. The spring pitchers are 20 to 50 cm long and curve backward. The summer pitchers are larger, 20 to 70 cm, and stand upright. The flowers are maroon, borne singly on stalks up to 60 cm tall, and open with the spring pitchers.

Where it grows

Permanently wet hillside seepage bogs, where a layer of clay near the surface holds the water. It cannot stand shade and depends on fire to keep its bogs open.

In Alabama

Found only in Alabama. The Service's 2012 review counted 11 surviving populations, six in Autauga County and five in Chilton County, and presumed the Elmore County sites lost. Seventeen populations had been destroyed since 1974.

Why it is at risk

Fire exclusion and the woody growth that follows, altered drainage, gravel mining, development, grazing, and roadside mowing. Poaching has been a problem in the past. Most sites are privately owned.

Notes

This plant was first described in 1974 as a full species, Sarracenia alabamensis, and some sources still use that name. The federal list treats it as a subspecies of Sarracenia rubra.
