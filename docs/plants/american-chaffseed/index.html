American chaffseed

Schwalbea americana

A hairy, purple-flowered herb of fire-kept pine savannas that takes part of its food from the roots of its neighbors.

What it looks like

An upright perennial, hairy throughout, with stems that branch only at the base and stalkless leaves that overlap in a spiral. The showy flowers are reddish-purple and five-lobed. It is a partial parasite: its roots fasten onto the roots of other plants.

Where it grows

Open, moist, acidic sandy or peaty ground in longleaf pine flatwoods and savannas kept open by fire.

In Alabama

Reported from Baldwin, Bullock, Geneva, and Mobile Counties.

Why it is at risk

Fire suppression, above all. Without regular burning, shrubs and trees close in and the plant disappears. Its seeds live only about a year in the soil, so there is no lasting seed bank to fall back on.
