Cylindrical lioplax

Lioplax cyclostomaformis

An olive-green snail that lives in pockets of mud under the big rocks of the Cahaba's shoals.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this snail.

What it looks like

A long shell, up to about 1.1 inches, olive-green outside and bluish inside the opening.

Where it lives

Small deposits of mud beneath large rocks, in the fast water of shoals.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Alabama, Black Warrior, Coosa, and Cahaba river systems. It is now known only from about 15 miles of the Cahaba River above the Fall Line, in Bibb and Shelby Counties.

Why it is at risk

Dams drowned the fast shoals it needs and cut its populations off from one another. Silt, pollution from runoff and wastewater, and water short of oxygen wear at what is left.
