All snails

Rough hornsnail

Pleurocera foremani

A long, knobbed snail of the Coosa River system, now down to a few short stretches of water.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this snail. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A long, thick, pyramid-shaped shell up to about 1.3 inches, with as many as nine yellowish-brown whorls. A double row of prominent knobs on the lower whorls sets it apart from other hornsnails.

Where it lives

Gravel, cobble, bedrock, and mud in moderate current, usually three to ten feet deep.

In Alabama

It lives only in the Coosa River system of Alabama. It once ranged along about 200 miles of the river and nine tributaries. It is now known from lower Yellowleaf Creek in Shelby County, from the Coosa River below Wetumpka in Elmore County, and from Mitchell Reservoir in Coosa and Chilton Counties.

Why it is at risk

Dams, which broke its range into pieces that cannot reach one another, along with runoff carrying silt and fertilizer, spills, drought, and the small size of what remains.
