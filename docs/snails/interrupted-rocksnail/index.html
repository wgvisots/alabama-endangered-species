All snails

Interrupted rocksnail

Leptoxis foremani

A snail of the Coosa's shoals that was thought extinct until 1997, and survives in a single reach of river in Georgia.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This snail is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this snail.

What it looks like

A small to medium snail with a thick, nearly round shell up to about an inch long, with a very low spire. It is dark brown to olive, sometimes spotted, and finely ridged.

Where it lives

Shoals and riffles of rivers, on rock, boulders, cobble, and gravel swept clean by moderate to strong current.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Coosa River from northwest Georgia down to Elmore County, and in lower Terrapin Creek in Cherokee County. It has been lost from more than 95 percent of that range. Hatchery-raised snails have been released in the Coosa in Alabama more than once, and none of those trials has taken hold.

Why it is at risk

The dams on the Coosa, which destroyed its shoals and still govern the flow and quality of the water, along with predation by fish.
