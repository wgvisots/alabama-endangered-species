Tulotoma

Tulotoma magnifica

A knobbed snail the size of a golf ball, moved from endangered to threatened in 2011 as it recovered.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this snail. A photo by Paul Johnson, Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources, on the FWS site.

What it looks like

A globe-shaped snail about the size of a golf ball, its shell ringed with spiral rows of knobs.

Where it lives

The undersides of large rocks, in shoals and runs with moderate to swift current.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Coosa River from St. Clair County down into the Alabama River as far as Clarke and Monroe Counties. It now lives in the Coosa River below Jordan Dam, in Elmore County, where the largest population is, and in six creeks that feed the Coosa.

Why it is at risk

The recovery plan lays the loss of more than 20 kinds of snail from the Coosa to the dams that turned the river into a chain of lakes. The tulotoma held on below the last dam and in the creeks.

Notes

Listed as endangered in 1991, and reclassified as threatened on June 2, 2011 (76 FR 31866), when the Service judged it to be well on the road to recovery.
