All snails

Slender campeloma

Campeloma decampi

A burrowing snail that bears live young, found only in a few creeks of the Tennessee Valley.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this snail. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A medium to large snail, up to about 1.4 inches long, with an egg-shaped, cone-like shell that tapers to a pointed spire and is marked with faint lines.

Where it lives

It burrows in soft sand, mud, and rotting leaves, in slow to moderate current.

How it lives

Like the other snails of its family, it bears live young instead of laying eggs. It does not seem to live more than three years.

In Alabama

It lives only in north Alabama, in creeks that run to the Tennessee River: Limestone, Piney, and Round Island Creeks in Limestone County, and, by the Service's account, Cypress Creek in Lauderdale County. Its range has shrunk by at least three-quarters, and Wheeler Reservoir stands between the populations that are left.

Why it is at risk

Runoff from farms, silt, and waste, which foul the water; pesticides from cotton fields; roads, railroads, and power lines that cross the creeks; and the clearing of land.
