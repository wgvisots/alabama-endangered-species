All snails

Plicate rocksnail

Leptoxis plicata

A rounded snail with a folded shell, found only in the shoals of the Locust Fork in Jefferson County.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this snail. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A rounded, fairly thick shell about three-quarters of an inch long, often with strong folds on the largest whorl. It is brown to dark yellowish-green, usually with four bands, and the opening is bluish-white.

Where it lives

Shallow shoals of gravel, cobble, and boulders in flowing water, often under large flat rocks.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Black Warrior, Little Warrior, and Tombigbee Rivers, and has been lost from more than 90 percent of that range. It now lives only along about 20 miles of the Locust Fork of the Black Warrior, in Jefferson County. More than 5,000 hatchery-raised snails were released there in 2011 and 2012, and they bred.

Why it is at risk

The damming of its rivers for navigation, along with poor water quality, silt, and pollution.
