All snails

Flat pebblesnail

Lepyrium showalteri

A flattened snail under a fifth of an inch across, clinging to smooth stones at two places in the Cahaba system.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this snail.

What it looks like

A very small snail with a flattened, oval shell, about a sixth of an inch high and a fifth of an inch wide, with a sunken spire.

Where it lives

Clean, smooth stones in the rapid current of river shoals, where it lays its eggs in capsules.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Coosa, Cahaba, and Little Cahaba Rivers. It is now known from one site on the Little Cahaba in Bibb County, and from one series of shoals on the Cahaba in Shelby County.

Why it is at risk

Dams drowned the fast shoals it needs and cut its populations off from one another. Silt, pollution from runoff and wastewater, and water short of oxygen wear at what is left.
