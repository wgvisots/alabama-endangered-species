Anthony's riversnail

Athearnia anthonyi

A heavy, knobbed snail of the Tennessee River, reduced to two populations, both in Alabama.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this snail. A photo by Paul Johnson, Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources, on the FWS site.

What it looks like

A thick, heavy, oval shell up to about an inch long, with a short spire and large, uneven bumps on the shoulder. It is yellowish green to dark brown, with bands that fade as the snail ages.

Where it lives

Flowing water in large rivers and the lower reaches of large creeks, over gravel, cobble, and boulders, and on sunken logs and plants.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Tennessee River from Knoxville down to Muscle Shoals. Two populations survive: one in the Tennessee River in Jackson County, and one in lower Limestone Creek, in Limestone County. In 2003, 4,000 snails were transplanted from Limestone Creek.

Why it is at risk

Dams flooded most of its river habitat, and poor use of the land harmed the tributaries.

Notes

A rule of June 14, 2001 (66 FR 32250) also set up an experimental population of this snail in the free-flowing reach of the Tennessee River below Wilson Dam.
