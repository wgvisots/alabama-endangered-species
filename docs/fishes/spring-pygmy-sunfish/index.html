Spring pygmy sunfish

Elassoma alabamae

A fish less than an inch long that lives a single year, among the water plants of a few springs in the Tennessee Valley.

What it looks like

A tiny fish, three-quarters of an inch to an inch long. Breeding males are dark brown, with five to seven narrow bars of silver or gold on each side. Females are brown above and mottled brown and white on the sides. Both have a golden crescent behind the eye.

Where it lives

Calm, clear spring pools and spring runs, thick with water plants.

How it lives

It spawns in March and April, laying its eggs among the plants, and most adults die afterward.

In Alabama

It lives only in the Tennessee River drainage of north Alabama, and is known today from two spring systems. The Service names the Beaverdam Creek and Spring system. A population at a spring in Lauderdale County appears to be gone, and one in Limestone County was brought back by landowners and biologists.

Why it is at risk

The pages read for this entry do not set out the threats; the listing rule, linked below, does. A fish that lives one year, in two spring systems, has little margin.
