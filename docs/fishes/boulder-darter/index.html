Boulder darter

Etheostoma wapiti

One of the rarest fish in the country, a darter of the fast, rocky water of the Elk River.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this fish. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A darter 1.7 to 2.8 inches long. Breeding males are olive to gray, with 10 to 14 fine stripes along the rear of the body and a blue throat.

Where it lives

Swift riffles and chutes of small to medium rivers. Adults have been found only over boulders and broken rock.

How it lives

It eats the larvae of insects and other small animals of the riffles. It probably spawns in late April and May.

In Alabama

In the wild it survives only in the Elk River, which enters Alabama in Limestone County, and in a tributary in Tennessee. It once lived in Shoal Creek, in Lauderdale County, where none had been found since 1884. In May 2005 the Service and its partners released 217 hatchery-raised young into Shoal Creek to bring it back.

Why it is at risk

Poor water quality and the building of Wilson Dam drove it from the Tennessee River and its other tributaries.

Notes

The fish released into Shoal Creek are an experimental population under a rule of January 6, 2005 (70 FR 1286). The sources read for this entry do not say how they have fared.
