Spotfin chub

Erimonax monachus

A minnow of clear upland rivers whose breeding males turn turquoise, last collected in Alabama in 1937.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This fish is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this fish. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A slender minnow 2.2 to 3.3 inches long, with a small triangular head, a mouth set low, and a barbel at each corner. Breeding males turn turquoise to royal blue on the back and upper sides, and the tips of their fins go milky white.

Where it lives

Clear upland rivers of middling size, in moderate to swift current over boulders and bedrock.

How it lives

It eats the young of water insects. It spawns from late May to August, in crevices among rocks.

In Alabama

Alabama has two old records: three fish from Shoal Creek near Florence in 1884, and one from Little Bear Creek near Tuscumbia in 1937. The state's own account calls its presence here today uncertain.

Why it is at risk

The sources read for this entry do not set out the threats.

Notes

A rule of January 6, 2005 (70 FR 1286) set up an experimental population of this fish, and of the boulder darter, in Shoal Creek, in Lauderdale County and across the line in Tennessee.
