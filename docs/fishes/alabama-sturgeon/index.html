Alabama sturgeon

Scaphirhynchus suttkusi

A small river sturgeon found only in the Mobile basin, and so rare that it may be close to extinction.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this fish. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A small, slender sturgeon about two and a half feet long, with a flattened, shovel-like snout, four barbels, and five rows of bony plates. The back is tan to golden or brownish-orange, and the belly creamy white.

Where it lives

Deep, fast currents of large rivers, over sand and gravel.

How it lives

It feeds mostly on the larvae of insects. It appears to spawn in late March or early April.

In Alabama

It once lived in all the large rivers of the Mobile basin below the Fall Line. It is now known only from the lower Alabama River, from R. F. Henry Lock and Dam down to the Tombigbee, and from the lower Cahaba. The last confirmed capture was in 2007, and the last sighting was of a live fish in April 2009.

Why it is at risk

Its numbers are so low that deaths outrun births. Overfishing in the past, dams, navigation works, and altered flows brought it to that point.

Notes

The state's page on the fish suggests that its survival may depend on spawning it in captivity.
