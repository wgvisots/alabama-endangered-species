All fishes

Pygmy sculpin

Cottus paulus

An inch-and-a-half fish whose entire range is one large spring near Anniston and the run below it.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this fish. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A small sculpin, about 1.5 inches long as an adult, gray to black, with three pale saddles across the back that are creamy white to pinkish.

Where it lives

A large spring of clear water at a constant temperature, thick with plants. The fish is most common in the gravel-bottomed run below it, in moderate to swift flow.

How it lives

It eats small snails, tiny crustaceans, and midge larvae. It breeds all year, most of all from April to August, and lays its eggs under stones.

In Alabama

Known only from Coldwater Spring and its spring run, a tributary of Choccolocco Creek in Calhoun County.

Why it is at risk

The pages read for this entry do not set out the threats. The spring is also the public water supply of Anniston.
