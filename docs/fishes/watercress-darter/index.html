Watercress darter

Etheostoma nuchale

A bright little darter that lives among the water plants of a few springs in and around Birmingham.

What it looks like

A darter 1 to 1.8 inches long, with up to nine saddles across the back and usually a pale stripe on the nape. Breeding males have a reddish-orange belly, a bright blue fin beneath the tail, and blue spots on the tail.

Where it lives

The deep, slow backwaters of springs, in thick beds of watercress and other water plants, where the water holds at 62 to 65 degrees all year.

How it lives

It eats the larvae of water insects and tiny crustaceans. It spawns from March through July.

In Alabama

The recovery plan of 1993 knew it from three springs in Jefferson County, all in the Black Warrior River system, and from one more where it had been introduced. The Service now counts five places in and around Birmingham.

Why it is at risk

Paving, which cuts the flow of water into the ground that feeds the springs; pumping; runoff from streets; and the chance of a chemical spill on the highways nearby. At one spring, grass carp stripped out the plants.
