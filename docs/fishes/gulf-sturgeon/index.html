Gulf sturgeon

Acipenser oxyrinchus desotoi

An armored giant that winters in the Gulf and climbs Alabama's coastal rivers each spring to spawn.

What it looks like

A very large fish that can reach about nine feet and 300 pounds, with rows of heavy bony plates along its body. The snout is V-shaped and often turned up at the tip. The back is light to medium brown and the belly cream.

Where it lives

It lives in salt water and spawns in fresh. From November through January it stays in estuaries and near the shore.

How it lives

The run upstream usually begins in February, and the fish spawn in the Choctawhatchee in April. The adults rest in deep pools through the summer and go back down in August or September. It seems to stop feeding in fresh water. It does not breed until it is 9 to 12 years old.

In Alabama

The best population in the state is in the Choctawhatchee River near Geneva. Sturgeon are also seen each year in the Conecuh, have been taken in the Mobile Delta, and are caught by accident in the Alabama and Tombigbee Rivers below their lowest dams.

Why it is at risk

Overfishing for caviar and meat began the decline. Dams that block the way to spawning grounds, dredging, capture in nets set for other fish, and pollution have kept it down.
