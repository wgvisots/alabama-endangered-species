Slackwater darter

Etheostoma boschungi

A darter of slow creeks that leaves them in winter to spawn in flooded fields and seeps.

What it looks like

A darter 1.6 to 2.4 inches long, with three distinct dark saddles across the back and dusky blotches under the head and body. Males have a bold bar below the eye, and in breeding season turn deep orange on the breast and cheeks.

Where it lives

For most of the year, gravel-bottomed pools of slow creeks and small rivers. To spawn, it moves into shallow water seeping through fields and woods that are usually dry in summer.

How it lives

It spawns from late January to March, fixing its eggs to plants. It eats tiny crustaceans and young water insects.

In Alabama

It lives in the Cypress Creek, Swan Creek, upper Shoal Creek, and Flint River systems of north Alabama. It was first described from a creek in Lauderdale County.

Why it is at risk
