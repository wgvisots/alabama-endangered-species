Alabama cavefish

Speoplatyrhinus poulsoni

A blind, colorless fish known from a single cave in Lauderdale County, and from nowhere else on earth.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this fish. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A small fish, 1.2 to 2.3 inches long, with no eyes and almost no color. Its head is long and flattened, with a narrow snout, and it has no pelvic fins. Ridges of sensory bumps run along the head and sides.

Where it lives

Very clear cave water. The fish has been seen near the surface of a pool about ten feet deep, below a roost of bats, whose droppings feed the cave's small community of crayfish and other animals.

How it lives

It is thought to live five to ten years, and its numbers are thought to grow more slowly than those of almost any other cave animal.

In Alabama

Known only from Key Cave, in Lauderdale County, which now lies within Key Cave National Wildlife Refuge. No count of the fish is given in the sources read, which call the population small.

Why it is at risk

Pollution of the groundwater, a falling water table, and any loss of the bats that feed the cave. Much of the land that drains into the cave's aquifer is unprotected.

Notes

First listed as threatened in 1977, and reclassified as endangered on September 28, 1988 (53 FR 37968).
