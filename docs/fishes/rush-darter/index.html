Rush darter

Etheostoma phytophilum

A plain little darter of spring-fed streams, known from three small, separate patches of north-central Alabama.

What it looks like

A small, brownish to yellow fish, two to three inches long, with no bright markings.

Where it lives

Small, clear, cool streams and spring runs, among reeds and rushes.

How it lives

It lives two to three years. It was not recognized as a species until 1999.

In Alabama

It lives only in Alabama, in three separate groups above the Fall Line: in the Clear Creek drainage of Winston County, in the Turkey Creek drainage of Jefferson County, and in the Little Cove Creek drainage of Etowah County. Most of the fish are on private land.

Why it is at risk

Silt and stormwater from spreading towns, changes made to the heads of springs, road work, and logging. The listing rule judged these threats to be both large and close at hand.
