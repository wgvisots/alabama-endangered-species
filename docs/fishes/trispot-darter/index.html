Trispot darter

Etheostoma trisella

A small darter thought lost from Alabama for half a century, until it turned up again in 2010.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this fish. A photo of a breeding male by Alan Cressler, on the FWS site (copyrighted, shown there by permission).

What it looks like

A small darter, 1.3 to 1.6 inches long, with a yellow-brown back crossed by three dark saddles, a pale belly, and a thin bar below the eye. Breeding males turn bright orange, with about four green blotches on each side.

Where it lives

Outside the breeding season, slow, clear backwaters and eddies over cobble and gravel. It breeds in water seeping through marsh grasses in nearby low ground.

How it lives

It spawns from January through March, and its sticky eggs cling to plants and stones. Adults live two to three years.

In Alabama

It was first described from a creek in Cherokee County that Weiss Reservoir flooded in 1960, and after that it was thought to be gone from the state. In 2010 it was found in a tributary of Big Canoe Creek, and in 2011 in a second.

Why it is at risk

The pages read for this entry do not set out the threats. The listing rule, linked below, does.

Notes

A special rule under section 4(d) of the Act, published in 2020 (85 FR 61614), sets out which activities are allowed around the fish.
