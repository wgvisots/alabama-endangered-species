Slenderclaw crayfish

Cambarus cracens

A small crayfish with long, slim claws, found only in a few streams on Sand Mountain.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this crustacean. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A fairly small crayfish with a long, slender front claw. The largest on record had a body shell 1.56 inches long. Some are plain olive green to rusty brown, and others are mottled, pale gray to straw with rusty red to dark brown speckles.

Where it lives

Small to medium flowing streams with boulders and cobble to shelter under and with their native streamside plants intact.

How it lives

Very little is known of how it lives. It is thought to mature in its first year and to live two to three years.

In Alabama

It lives only in two watersheds on Sand Mountain, in DeKalb and Marshall Counties, in streams that flow to the Tennessee River at Guntersville Lake. Five sites are known today. It has not been found since the 1970s at the place where it was first collected, or at three other old sites.

Why it is at risk

Chiefly the virile crayfish, Faxonius virilis, a species from elsewhere that is moving into its streams. Pollution and runoff from farms, livestock, and unpaved roads also harm its habitat.
