Alabama cave shrimp

Palaemonias alabamae

A colorless, nearly transparent shrimp that lives in the pools of a few caves around Huntsville.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this crustacean. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A small shrimp, up to 0.8 inch long, without color and largely transparent. It looks much like the Kentucky cave shrimp, which is also endangered, but is smaller and has a shorter beak.

Where it lives

Pools in caves.

In Alabama

Two populations are confirmed, in two separate groundwater basins. One is in a cave on Redstone Arsenal, in Madison County, and the other is in a nearby cave system. Shrimp have been seen in two more caves, and it is not clear whether those are populations of their own. The shrimp has not been seen since the early 1970s in the Madison County cave where it was first found, and a report from western Jackson County has not been confirmed.

Why it is at risk

Polluted runoff, which steadily fouls the water of the caves, and building and forest clearing on the land that drains into them. Water tests at one cave suggest that the deep groundwater may be polluted as well.
