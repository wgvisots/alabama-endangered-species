Eastern indigo snake

Drymarchon couperi

A large, glossy, blue-black snake of the sandhills that winters in gopher tortoise burrows.

What it looks like

A large, thick-bodied snake, glossy blue-black all over, with a chin and throat that may be reddish, orange-brown, or cream. The young are black with narrow whitish-blue bands. It is not venomous.

Where it lives

Longleaf pine sandhills, scrub, pine flatwoods, hardwood hammocks, and coastal dunes. It shelters in the burrows of gopher tortoises.

How it lives

It breeds from October through January, and the adults roam over large home ranges. It lives 8 to 12 years in the wild.

In Alabama

Once a snake of Alabama's coastal plain. The Encyclopedia of Alabama reports that it has been released in Conecuh National Forest to bring it back to the state, and that no established breeding population was known when that article was written.

Why it is at risk

Loss and breaking up of habitat by building and roads, vehicle strikes, killing by people, and a rising sea.
