Escambia map turtle

Graptemys ernsti

A large map turtle of the rivers of south Alabama, listed because it is hard to tell from a rarer cousin.

Listed for its likeness, not its rarity. This reptile is on the federal list only because it is hard to tell from a species that is in danger.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this reptile.

What it looks like

A large river turtle. The females reach about 11 inches and the males about 5. The shell is olive green to tan, with light yellow markings shaped like circles or the letter C, which fade in old females.

Where it lives

Rivers only, and most of all where there are logs and sandbars.

In Alabama

Recorded in the Conecuh, Yellow, Pea, and Choctawhatchee Rivers.

Why it is at risk

It is not judged to be in danger itself. It was listed because it is so hard to tell from the Pearl River map turtle of Mississippi and Louisiana, which collectors take illegally. Collecting it is now banned.
