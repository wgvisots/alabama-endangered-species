Hawksbill sea turtle

Eretmochelys imbricata

A reef turtle with a hawk-like beak, hunted for its shell, and a rare stray in Alabama's waters.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this reptile. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A small to medium sea turtle, 2.5 to 3 feet long, with a long oval shell whose plates overlap like shingles, a beak like a hawk's, and two claws on each flipper. The shell is brown, splashed with yellow, orange, or red.

Where it lives

Rocky places, coral reefs, shallow coastal waters, and lagoons, seldom deeper than 65 feet. It nests on quiet tropical beaches.

How it lives

It eats mostly sponges.

In Alabama

The Service's Alabama office counts the hawksbill among the sea turtles it answers for. It is a turtle of tropical reefs, and the state's guide to its sea turtles does not mention it.

Why it is at risk

Hunting for tortoiseshell, which goes on through illegal trade, along with coastal development, lights on nesting beaches, pollution, boat strikes, and capture in fishing gear.
