Gopher tortoise

Gopherus polyphemus

A burrowing land turtle whose long tunnels shelter hundreds of other animals.

What it looks like

A large, dark brown to grayish-black land turtle with a shell 6 to 15 inches long. Its hind feet are stumpy, like an elephant's, and its front feet are flattened like shovels for digging. The lower shell is yellowish and has no hinge.

Where it lives

Open pine woods on sandy, well-drained soil, kept open by fire.

How it lives

It digs burrows that are often more than 30 feet long. About 60 kinds of vertebrates and 300 kinds of invertebrates take shelter in them. A tortoise can live 50 to 80 years.

In Alabama

Only the tortoises west of the Mobile and Tombigbee Rivers are on the federal list, and in Alabama that is the southwest corner of the state. East of those rivers the tortoise is not federally listed.

Why it is at risk

Loss, breaking up, and decline of its habitat, much of it from the loss of fire and of open pine woods.
