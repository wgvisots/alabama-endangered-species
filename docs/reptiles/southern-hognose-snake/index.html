Southern hognose snake

Heterodon simus

A small, stout snake with an upturned snout, proposed for listing as threatened, and not recorded in Alabama since 1975.

Proposed, not listed. This reptile has no protection under the Endangered Species Act unless a final rule lists it.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this reptile. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A short, stout snake, usually 14 to 21 inches long, with a sharply upturned, pointed snout. It is yellowish gray or light brown, tinged with orange-red on the back, with large, dark, squarish blotches down the spine and smaller ones on the sides. The belly is cream. It is not venomous.

Where it lives

Dry uplands of the coastal plain and sandhills: old fields, openings in the woods, and hardwood hammocks.

How it lives

It is secretive, and spends much of its time under litter or underground. It eats mostly toads, which it digs out with its snout. When threatened it hisses, flattens its neck, and plays dead. It does not bite.

In Alabama

It was once found in the Ridge and Valley country of central Alabama and in parts of the coastal plain. The state knows of no record since 1975. It may be gone from Alabama, or it may hang on in very low numbers.

Why it is at risk

It is declining throughout its range, and the state's account says the reasons are not known.

Notes

The Service proposed listing it as threatened on August 29, 2025, and reopened the proposal for comment on June 8, 2026 (91 FR 34597). No final rule had been published when this site was last checked.
