Alabama red-bellied turtle

Pseudemys alabamensis

A large plant-eating turtle of the Mobile-Tensaw Delta.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this reptile. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A large freshwater turtle with a shell 8 to 10 inches long, tall and arched, brown to olive with light upright bars. The belly is usually orange to reddish. The upper jaw has a notch at its tip with a tooth-like point on each side.

Where it lives

Shallow, weedy backwaters and river channels. It depends on underwater plants for food, shelter, and places to bask.

How it lives

It feeds almost wholly on water plants.

In Alabama

It lives around Mobile Bay and in the Mobile-Tensaw Delta, mostly in Baldwin and Mobile Counties. Its main nesting ground in the state is Gravine Island, a degraded dredge-spoil site in the delta. The Alabama population appears stable, but is not growing.

Why it is at risk

Dredging, pollution, development, bulkheads that block the way to nesting ground, predators at the nests, boat strikes, shooting, and deaths on roads.
