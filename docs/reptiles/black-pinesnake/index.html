Black pinesnake

Pituophis melanoleucus lodingi

A large black constrictor of the longleaf pine country of southwest Alabama.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this reptile. A photo by Kevin Narum, The Nature Conservancy, on the FWS site.

What it looks like

A snake that is black or dark brown above and below. Some have a russet snout, white scales on the neck, or faint blotches toward the tail. It is not venomous, and kills its prey by constriction.

Where it lives

Longleaf pine forest.

In Alabama

Found in Clarke, Mobile, and Washington Counties.

Why it is at risk

Loss of longleaf forest, snake fungal disease, vehicle strikes, and deliberate killing. The populations that remain are small and cut off from one another, and inbreeding has begun to harm their health.
