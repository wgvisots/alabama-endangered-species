Barbour's map turtle

Graptemys barbouri

A big-headed map turtle of the rivers of southeast Alabama, listed because it is hard to tell from a rarer cousin.

Listed for its likeness, not its rarity. This reptile is on the federal list only because it is hard to tell from a species that is in danger.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this reptile. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A large river turtle. The females are 8 to 12 inches long, with massive heads, and the males seldom pass 5 inches. The shell is olive to tan, with light yellow markings shaped like circles or the letter C.

Where it lives

Rivers only, and most of all stretches with exposed limestone and with snags to bask on.

In Alabama

It lives in the large rivers of the southeast, chiefly the Chattahoochee and Choctawhatchee systems.

Why it is at risk

It is not judged to be in danger itself. It was listed because it is so hard to tell from the Pearl River map turtle of Mississippi and Louisiana, which collectors take illegally. Collecting it is now banned.
