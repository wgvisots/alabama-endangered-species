Alabama map turtle

Graptemys pulchra

A river turtle named for the fine, map-like lines on its shell, listed because it is hard to tell from a rarer cousin.

Listed for its likeness, not its rarity. This reptile is on the federal list only because it is hard to tell from a species that is in danger.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this reptile.

What it looks like

A medium-sized turtle. The females are 5.5 to 11.5 inches long and the males 3.5 to 5. The brown shell bears faint orange, yellow, and green markings that look like the lines of a map.

Where it lives

Rivers and streams with sandy or muddy bottoms and with places to bask.

In Alabama

It lives in the Alabama, Tombigbee, Tensaw, Black Warrior, and Coosa Rivers and the streams that feed them.

Why it is at risk

It is not judged to be in danger itself. It was listed because it is so hard to tell from the Pearl River map turtle of Mississippi and Louisiana, which collectors take illegally. Collecting it is now banned.
