All reptiles

Alligator snapping turtle

Macrochelys temminckii

The largest freshwater turtle in North America, proposed for listing as threatened.

Proposed, not listed. This reptile has no protection under the Endangered Species Act unless a final rule lists it.

What it looks like

A very large turtle with massive jaws and a rough, ridged shell that recalls the hide of an alligator. Males can reach 249 pounds. The females are much smaller.

Where it lives

Fresh water.

In Alabama

The Service's Alabama office counts it among the species it answers for. The sources read for this entry do not name the rivers.

Why it is at risk

Harvest for meat, which emptied many rivers of large turtles, along with collecting, predators at the nests, drowning in fishing gear, swallowed hooks, and changes to its rivers.

Notes

The Service proposed listing it as threatened in November 2021. It has no row on the federal list as of the date this site was checked.
