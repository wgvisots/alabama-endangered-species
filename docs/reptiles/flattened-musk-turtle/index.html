Flattened musk turtle

Sternotherus depressus

A small turtle with a flattened shell, found only in the upper Black Warrior River system.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this reptile. A photo by James Godwin, Alabama Natural Heritage Program, on the FWS site (copyrighted, shown there by permission).

What it looks like

A small aquatic turtle with a distinctly flattened shell, up to 4.7 inches long. The shell is dark brown to orange, and the underside pink to yellowish. It has two barbels on the chin and webbed feet.

Where it lives

Free-flowing creeks and small rivers, with shallows and with deeper pools swept by the current, where rocks and crevices give shelter.

How it lives

It eats snails, mussels, clams, and water insects. The adults are active mostly at night, from April to October. Females do not breed until they are six to eight years old.

In Alabama

Found only in Alabama, in the Black Warrior River system upstream of Bankhead Dam. It now holds an estimated quarter to a third of its former range.

Why it is at risk

Silt is the greatest threat. Dams, development, and collecting for the pet trade have also cut its numbers.
