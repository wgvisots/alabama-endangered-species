Loggerhead sea turtle

Caretta caretta

The sea turtle Alabama sees most, and the only one that nests here every year.

What it looks like

A large sea turtle. Adults average about 36 inches in shell length and 250 pounds. The shell is reddish-brown to brown, and the underside yellow to brown.

Where it lives

The open Gulf and coastal waters. The females come ashore on sandy beaches to nest.

How it lives

A female lays about 80 to 120 eggs in a nest, and they hatch after about two months.

In Alabama

Loggerheads nest from the Florida line to Dauphin Island, most of them between Fort Morgan and Gulf Shores.

Why it is at risk

On Alabama's beaches the chief danger is light. Bright lights confuse nesting females and draw hatchlings away from the water, where they die of exhaustion, drying, or predators. Shield or turn off lights that can be seen from the beach, and never use a white flashlight near a nest.
