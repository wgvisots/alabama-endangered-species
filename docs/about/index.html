About and sources

This site gathers, in one place, what the public record says about the plants and animals in Alabama that are protected under the federal Endangered Species Act.

What is covered

157 entries: 9 mammals, 6 birds, 16 reptiles, 5 amphibians, 17 fishes, 63 mussels, 13 snails, 2 crustaceans, 3 insects, and 23 plants. Under the Act, an endangered species is in danger of extinction, and a threatened species is likely to become endangered.

Four kinds of entry appear, and each group's page keeps them apart.

  • Most are species on the federal list that live in Alabama today.
  • Some are on the federal list and once lived in Alabama, but are thought to be gone from the state. They are marked as such.
  • A few are on the list only because they are hard to tell from a species that is in danger. The alligator and three map turtles are the cases here.
  • Some have only been proposed for listing. A proposal protects nothing until a final rule follows, and those entries say so.

A few listed animals are in Alabama as experimental populations, released to bring a species back, which the Act treats under looser rules. The whooping crane is one. The notes on several mussels, two fishes, and one snail describe others.

Not covered: whales, sea-going fishes, and other animals of the open Gulf, which fall to NOAA Fisheries and not to the Fish & Wildlife Service; candidates that have not reached a proposal; and the many plants and animals Alabama tracks as rare that have no federal status.

Species that are no longer on the federal list are left out as well. The wood stork and the snail darter are two. So are several mussels that older lists for Alabama still carry, among them the flat pigtoe, the stirrupshell, the southern acornshell, and the upland combshell.

How it was put together

  1. The plants, and the months each one flowers, come from the list kept by the Fish & Wildlife Service's Alabama field office.
  2. The animals come from the Service's list of the species its Alabama office answers for, read beside the Encyclopedia of Alabama's account of the state's listed animals and the state's own list of its mussels. Those two supplied most of the listed species that have been lost from Alabama.
  3. Each species' status, listing date, and Federal Register citation were checked against the federal lists themselves, 50 CFR 17.11 for wildlife and 17.12 for plants. The listing date is the day the final rule was published. Proposed species were checked against the Service's own list of species proposed for listing.
  4. Descriptions, habitats, and threats were written from Fish & Wildlife Service species profiles, recovery plans, status assessments, five-year reviews, and listing rules, and from the species accounts of the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources. Each entry's references name what was used.
  5. Alabama counties and rivers come from a federal document where one names them. Otherwise they come from the Encyclopedia of Alabama for plants, the Alabama Butterfly Atlas for butterflies, and the state's accounts for other animals.

Every entry links to the documents it was drawn from. The whole site was last checked against them on November 11, 2026.

What to keep in mind

  • Listings change. A species can be added, removed, or reclassified after the date above.
  • Some of the sources are old. Many of the mussel and snail entries rest on recovery plans written around 2000, and a stream named in one of them may have lost its animals, or regained them, since.
  • An entry says only what its sources say. Where they describe no threats, or do not describe the animal, the entry says so or leaves the heading out. Where they disagree, the entry gives both accounts.
  • County lists mix current and older records. A county's name on an entry does not mean the species still lives there, and it never means it is easy to find.
  • Places are given by county, river, or creek. Exact sites, and the names of caves and most springs, are left out on purpose, because rare plants are dug up, and rare animals taken, by collectors.
  • This is not an identification guide. Many of these species have common look-alikes, and telling them apart can take a specialist.
  • Scientists and the federal list do not always use the same name. Entries are filed under the name on the federal list and give the others.
  • If you find one of these species, photograph it and leave it where it is. Federal law restricts collecting and selling listed species.

Sources used throughout

  • U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, Alabama Ecological Services Field Office: Alabama Listed Plants fws.gov
    The list of plants and their flowering periods.
  • U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service: Species Point of Contact in Alabama fws.gov
    The animals the Service's Alabama office answers for, group by group.
  • Code of Federal Regulations, 50 CFR Part 17: the Lists of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants ecfr.gov
    Each species' status, listing date, and Federal Register citation.
  • 50 CFR 17.12, the plant list, as published by the Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School law.cornell.edu
    The copy of the federal plant list that was read for this site.
  • 50 CFR 17.11, the wildlife list, as published by the Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School law.cornell.edu
    The copy of the federal wildlife list that was read for this site.
  • U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, ECOS: Species Proposed for Listing ecos.fws.gov
    Which species have been proposed for listing and are not yet on the list.
  • Encyclopedia of Alabama: Endangered and Threatened Plants of Alabama, by L. J. Davenport, C. Oberholster, and B. R. Keener encyclopediaofalabama.org
    Alabama counties for plants, where a federal document does not give them.
  • Encyclopedia of Alabama: Endangered and Threatened Animal Species of Alabama encyclopediaofalabama.org
    Which listed animals once lived in Alabama and are thought to be gone.
  • Outdoor Alabama, the site of the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources outdooralabama.com
    Descriptions of many animals, and where in Alabama they are found.
  • Alabama Butterfly Atlas alabama.butterflyatlas.usf.edu
    Alabama counties, flight seasons, and habits for butterflies.
  • Alabama Plant Atlas floraofalabama.org
    County maps and specimen photos for every plant in the state. Further reading.
  • Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources: Alabama Plant Ranking List outdooralabama.com
    State conservation ranks, including many rare plants that are not on the federal list. Further reading.

Privacy

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and loads no outside scripts or fonts. Your browser fetches the photos from fws.gov, so that server sees the request for the image, though it is not told which page asked.
