About and sources

This site gathers, in one place, what the public record says about the plants and animals in Alabama that are protected under the federal Endangered Species Act. It is being built one group at a time.

What is covered

So far: 23 plants and 3 insects. Under the Act, an endangered species is in danger of extinction, and a threatened species is likely to become endangered.

Three kinds of entry appear, and each group's page keeps them apart.

  • Most are species on the federal list that live in Alabama today.
  • Some are on the federal list and once lived in Alabama, but are thought to be gone from the state. They are marked as such.
  • A few have only been proposed for listing. A proposal protects nothing until a final rule follows, and those entries say so.

Not covered yet: the other groups of animals, candidates that have not reached a proposal, and the many plants and animals Alabama tracks as rare that have no federal status.

How it was put together

  1. The plants, and the months each one flowers, come from the list kept by the Fish & Wildlife Service's Alabama field office.
  2. The insects come from the Service's list of the species its Alabama office answers for. The Encyclopedia of Alabama supplied the listed insect that has been lost from the state.
  3. Each species' status, listing date, and Federal Register citation were checked against the federal lists themselves, 50 CFR 17.11 for wildlife and 17.12 for plants. The listing date is the day the final rule was published.
  4. Descriptions, habitats, and threats were written from Fish & Wildlife Service species profiles, recovery plans, status assessments, and five-year reviews. Where those were thin, other sources filled the gap, and the entry's references name them.
  5. Alabama counties come from a federal document where one names them. Otherwise they come from the Encyclopedia of Alabama for plants and the Alabama Butterfly Atlas for butterflies.

Every entry links to the documents it was drawn from. The whole site was last checked against them on November 11, 2026.

What to keep in mind

  • Listings change. A species can be added, removed, or reclassified after the date above.
  • County lists mix current and older records. A county's name on an entry does not mean the species still lives there, and it never means it is easy to find.
  • Places are given by county or river only. Exact sites are left out on purpose, because rare plants are dug up, and rare butterflies netted, by collectors.
  • This is not an identification guide. Many of these species have common look-alikes, and telling them apart can take a specialist.
  • Scientists and the federal list do not always use the same name. Entries are filed under the name on the federal list and give the others.
  • If you find one of these species, photograph it and leave it where it is. Federal law restricts collecting and selling listed species.

Sources used throughout

  • U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, Alabama Ecological Services Field Office: Alabama Listed Plants fws.gov
    The list of plants and their flowering periods.
  • U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service: Species Point of Contact in Alabama fws.gov
    The animals the Service's Alabama office answers for, group by group.
  • Code of Federal Regulations, 50 CFR Part 17: the Lists of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants ecfr.gov
    Each species' status, listing date, and Federal Register citation.
  • 50 CFR 17.12, the plant list, as published by the Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School law.cornell.edu
    The copy of the federal plant list that was read for this site.
  • 50 CFR 17.11, the wildlife list, as published by the Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School law.cornell.edu
    The copy of the federal wildlife list that was read for this site.
  • Encyclopedia of Alabama: Endangered and Threatened Plants of Alabama, by L. J. Davenport, C. Oberholster, and B. R. Keener encyclopediaofalabama.org
    Alabama counties for plants, where a federal document does not give them.
  • Encyclopedia of Alabama: Endangered and Threatened Animal Species of Alabama encyclopediaofalabama.org
    Which listed animals once lived in Alabama and are thought to be gone.
  • Alabama Butterfly Atlas alabama.butterflyatlas.usf.edu
    Alabama counties, flight seasons, and habits for butterflies.
  • Alabama Plant Atlas floraofalabama.org
    County maps and specimen photos for every plant in the state. Further reading.
  • Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources: Alabama Plant Ranking List outdooralabama.com
    State conservation ranks, including many rare plants that are not on the federal list. Further reading.
