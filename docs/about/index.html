About and sources

This site gathers, in one place, what the public record says about the plants and animals in Alabama that are protected under the federal Endangered Species Act. It begins with the plants.

What is covered

The 23 plants here are the ones the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service's Alabama field office lists as federally protected in the state: 14 endangered and 9 threatened. Under the Act, an endangered species is in danger of extinction, and a threatened species is likely to become endangered.

Not covered yet: the state's listed animals, plants that are candidates for listing, and the many plants Alabama tracks as rare that have no federal status.

How it was put together

  1. The list of plants, and the months each one flowers, come from the Fish & Wildlife Service's Alabama field office.
  2. Each plant's status, listing date, and Federal Register citation were checked against the federal list itself, 50 CFR 17.12. The listing date is the day the final rule was published.
  3. Descriptions, habitats, and threats were written from Fish & Wildlife Service species profiles, recovery plans, status assessments, and five-year reviews. Where those were thin, Wikipedia filled the gap, and the entry's references say so.
  4. Alabama counties come from a federal document where one names them, and otherwise from the Encyclopedia of Alabama.

Every entry links to the documents it was drawn from. The whole site was last checked against them on November 12, 2026.

What to keep in mind

  • Listings change. A plant can be added, removed, or reclassified after the date above.
  • County lists mix current and older records. A county's name on an entry does not mean the plant still grows there, and it never means the plant is easy to find.
  • Places are given by county or river only. Exact sites are left out on purpose, because rare plants are dug up by collectors.
  • This is not an identification guide. Many of these plants have common look-alikes, and telling them apart can take a botanist.
  • Botanists and the federal list do not always use the same name for a plant. Entries are filed under the name on the federal list and give the others.
  • If you find one of these plants, photograph it and leave it where it is. Federal law restricts collecting and selling listed plants.

Sources used throughout

  • U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, Alabama Ecological Services Field Office: Alabama Listed Plants fws.gov
    The list of plants and their flowering periods.
  • Code of Federal Regulations, 50 CFR 17.12: List of Endangered and Threatened Plants ecfr.gov
    Each plant's status, listing date, and Federal Register citation.
  • 50 CFR 17.12 as published by the Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School law.cornell.edu
    The copy of the federal list that was read for this site.
  • Encyclopedia of Alabama: Endangered and Threatened Plants of Alabama, by L. J. Davenport, C. Oberholster, and B. R. Keener encyclopediaofalabama.org
    Alabama counties, where a federal document does not give them.
  • Alabama Plant Atlas floraofalabama.org
    County maps and specimen photos for every plant in the state. Further reading.
  • Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources: Alabama Plant Ranking List outdooralabama.com
    State conservation ranks, including many rare plants that are not on the federal list. Further reading.

Privacy

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and loads no outside scripts or fonts. Your browser fetches the photos from fws.gov, so that server sees the request for the image, though it is not told which page asked.
