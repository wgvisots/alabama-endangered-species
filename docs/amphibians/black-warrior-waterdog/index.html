Black Warrior waterdog

Necturus alabamensis

A large salamander that keeps its gills for life, found only in the streams of the upper Black Warrior basin.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this amphibian. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A large aquatic salamander up to 9.5 inches long, with a flattened head and body, feathery gills on the outside, and four toes on each foot. It is usually brown, sometimes spotted, with a dark stripe through the eye.

Where it lives

Medium and large streams with bottoms of clay or bedrock and little sand, and with rock crevices and slabs for shelter and for laying eggs.

In Alabama

Found only in Alabama, in streams above the Fall Line in the Black Warrior River basin. It is known from 14 scattered sites in the Sipsey Fork, Locust Fork, Mulberry Fork, North River, and their tributaries, in Blount, Tuscaloosa, Walker, and Winston Counties.

Why it is at risk

Silt, above all. Heavy siltation fills the crevices it shelters in, cuts its food, and smothers its nests and eggs.
