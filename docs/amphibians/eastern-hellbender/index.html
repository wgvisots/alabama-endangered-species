All amphibians

Eastern hellbender

Cryptobranchus alleganiensis alleganiensis

The largest salamander in North America, a stream dweller of north Alabama proposed for listing as endangered.

Proposed, not listed. This amphibian has no protection under the Endangered Species Act unless a final rule lists it.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this amphibian. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A salamander up to two feet long, with a flattened body, a keeled tail, tiny eyes, and loose folds of skin along its sides, through which it breathes.

Where it lives

Cool, clear streams and rivers that flow all year. Adults shelter under large flat rocks, and the young hide under smaller rocks and in gravel.

How it lives

The male makes a nest under a rock, guards the eggs, and may stay on to guard the hatchlings.

In Alabama

The Service places northern Alabama within its range of 15 states. The sources read for this entry do not name the streams.

Why it is at risk

Silt in streams, poor water quality, loss of habitat, disease, and collecting for the pet trade. Of 626 populations on record, 41 percent are believed gone, and most of the rest are declining.

Notes

The Missouri population was listed as endangered in 2021. The Service proposed listing the eastern hellbender throughout its range on December 13, 2024. No final rule has followed, so Alabama's hellbenders have no protection under the Act today.
