All amphibians

Red Hills salamander

Phaeognathus hubrichti

Alabama's state amphibian, a large burrowing salamander that lives nowhere else on earth.

What it looks like

A large, dark brown salamander about 8 inches long, with no markings, a long body, and short legs. It has no lungs and breathes through its moist skin.

Where it lives

Mature hardwood forest with a closed canopy, on steep slopes and in moist ravines, where ledges of limestone lie over siltstone that holds water. It lives in small burrows and cracks in that rock.

How it lives

It lives about ten years and breeds slowly, laying 6 to 10 eggs in its burrow. The young hatch as small salamanders, with no stage in the water.

In Alabama

Its whole range is about 60,000 acres of the Red Hills, between the Alabama River on the west and the Conecuh River on the east, in Butler, Conecuh, Covington, Crenshaw, Monroe, and Wilcox Counties.

Why it is at risk

The Service's profile does not list threats. The salamander's small range, slow breeding, and need for mature, shaded forest leave it little margin.

Notes

The Encyclopedia of Alabama notes that it was the first amphibian in North America to receive federal protection.
