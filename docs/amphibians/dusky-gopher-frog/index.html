All amphibians

Dusky gopher frog

Rana sevosa

A stout, warty frog of the longleaf pinelands, recorded in Alabama only once, in 1922.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This amphibian is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this amphibian.

What it looks like

A plump frog with a large head and a warty back, 2.2 to 4.1 inches long. Its color runs from nearly black to brown, with reddish spots. The females are the larger.

Where it lives

Longleaf pine uplands. It breeds in isolated seasonal ponds that hold no predatory fish.

In Alabama

Alabama's only record is a sighting of three frogs near Mobile Bay, in Mobile County, in 1922. No breeding pond has ever been found in the state.

Why it is at risk

Loss and breaking up of the pinelands, changes to the water in its breeding ponds, disease, predators, and the small size of the populations that remain.
