Reticulated flatwoods salamander

Ambystoma bishopi

A slender, net-patterned salamander of the longleaf flatwoods, last seen in Alabama in 1981.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This amphibian is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this amphibian. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A slender salamander, black to chocolate-black, with a net of light gray markings.

Where it lives

Longleaf pine flatwoods and savannas. It breeds in seasonal wetlands there and spends most of its life in the uplands nearby.

In Alabama

Alabama has old records from Mobile, Baldwin, Covington, and Houston Counties. The last sighting in the state was in 1981, and the salamander is no longer known here.

Why it is at risk

The loss of fire, which lets the flatwoods and their breeding ponds grow over, along with loss of habitat, predatory fish, fire ants, disease, drought, and a rising sea.
