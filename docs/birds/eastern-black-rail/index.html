Eastern black rail

Laterallus jamaicensis jamaicensis

A sparrow-sized marsh bird, the smallest rail in North America, and one almost never seen.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this bird. A photo by Christy Hand, South Carolina Department of Natural Resources, on the FWS site (copyrighted, shown there by permission).

What it looks like

A tiny rail, 4 to 6 inches long, gray-black with white speckles on the back, a chestnut nape, a short tail, red eyes, and a black bill.

Where it lives

Dense marsh cover over moist soil or very shallow water, in salt, brackish, and fresh marshes.

How it lives

It is secretive. It runs through the vegetation where another bird would fly, and the males may call by day or by night.

In Alabama

Alabama lies within its range on the Gulf coast, but the Service found no credible records from the state between 2010 and 2017. Texas and Florida are its strongholds on the Gulf.

Why it is at risk

Loss and breaking up of marshes, changes to their water, a rising sea, contaminants, predators, and disturbance by people.
