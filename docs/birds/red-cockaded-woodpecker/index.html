Red-cockaded woodpecker

Dryobates borealis

A small woodpecker of old, open pine forest, and the only one that carves its home in living pines.

What it looks like

A small black-and-white woodpecker about 9 inches long, with a barred back, white underparts spotted black along the flanks, a black crown, and large white cheek patches. The male has a small red mark near the crown that is seldom seen.

Where it lives

Mature, open pine forest, mostly longleaf, with trees usually more than 80 years old.

How it lives

It lives in small family groups that raise the young together. It digs its nest cavity in a living pine, often one softened by red-heart disease, and the work can take years. It keeps resin flowing around the hole, which wards off rat snakes.

In Alabama

It lives in Alabama where mature, open pine forest remains. The state's wildlife agency notes that its numbers fell sharply as the longleaf forests were cut, and that biologists now add artificial nest boxes to help it. The sources read for this entry do not name the places, so see the status assessment for the populations.

Why it is at risk

Loss of mature pine forest.

Notes

Listed as endangered in 1970, and reclassified as threatened by a rule published October 25, 2024 (89 FR 85337).
