All birds

Piping plover

Charadrius melodus

A small, sand-colored shorebird that winters on the islands at the mouth of Mobile Bay.

What it looks like

A small shorebird about 7 inches long, sandy brown above and white below, with orange legs. In breeding dress it has a black band across the forehead, another across the breast, and an orange bill tipped black. In winter, when Alabama sees it, the bands are gone and the bill is black.

Where it lives

Sandy beaches and tidal flats on the coast. Inland, it nests on the bare shores of lakes and on river sandbars.

How it lives

It picks small invertebrates from the surface of the sand. The chicks feed themselves from the day they hatch.

In Alabama

A winter visitor. The chief sites are Pelican Island, Little Dauphin Island, and parts of Dauphin Island, with occasional birds at the western tip of the Fort Morgan Peninsula. Fewer than 40 usually winter in the state at once, where more than 200 once did.

Why it is at risk

In Alabama: loss of habitat, disturbance by people, predators, and the decline of the tidal flats where it feeds.

Notes

The birds of the Great Lakes are listed separately, as endangered. Those from the Atlantic coast and the northern Great Plains are threatened.
