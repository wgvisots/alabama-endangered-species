Ivory-billed woodpecker

Campephilus principalis

The great woodpecker of the old southern forests, lost from Alabama and not surely seen anywhere since 1944.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This bird is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

What it looks like

A very large black-and-white woodpecker, about 19 to 20 inches long with a wingspan of 30 inches or more. It had a white, chisel-tipped bill, a lemon-yellow eye, and a pointed crest, red in the male and black in the female.

Where it lives

Very large forests with many big, dying trees.

In Alabama

Alabama's wildlife agency counts the ivory-bill among the woodpeckers lost from the state. The sources read for this entry do not say where in Alabama it lived or when it was last seen here.

Why it is at risk

The Service gives its last accepted sighting as April 1944, in the Singer Tract of northeast Louisiana. It needed forest of a size and age that is all but gone.
