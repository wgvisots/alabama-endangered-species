All birds

Whooping crane

Grus americana

The tallest bird in North America, a few of which now winter each year at Wheeler National Wildlife Refuge.

An experimental population. The birds in Alabama belong to a population released to bring the species back, which the Act treats under looser rules than it does the species elsewhere.

What it looks like

A white crane about 5 feet tall, with a wingspan of more than 7 feet, black wingtips, a red crown, and a dark gray patch on the nape.

Where it lives

Marshes, shallow lakes and bays, wet meadows, rivers, and farm fields.

How it lives

Whooping cranes pair for life and can live more than 30 years in the wild. A pair usually lays two eggs.

In Alabama

Whooping cranes have wintered at Wheeler National Wildlife Refuge every year since 2006, when two birds from a reintroduced flock turned up there. As many as 29 were counted in the winter of 2017 to 2018. They are often seen near the refuge's visitor center.

Why it is at risk

Loss of wetlands, development, power lines, wind farms, and drought.

Notes

The species as a whole has been listed as endangered since March 11, 1967 (32 FR 4001). The cranes that reach Alabama belong to the eastern migratory flock, which was set up in 2001 as a nonessential experimental population under a special rule.
