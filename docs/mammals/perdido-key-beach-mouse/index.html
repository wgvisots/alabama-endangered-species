Perdido Key beach mouse

Peromyscus polionotus trissyllepsis

A beach mouse confined to Perdido Key, the barrier island that Alabama shares with Florida.

What it looks like

A small mouse with large ears and protruding eyes. The head and body are about 3 inches long and the tail about 2. It is grayish fawn to wood brown above and white below, with the white reaching up to the eyes and ears.

Where it lives

Frontal dunes covered in sea oats, and the scrub dunes behind them. The two need to stay connected.

How it lives

It is active at night, and lives mostly on the seeds of sea oats and beach grass. When seeds run short, in late winter and early spring, it turns to insects and the fruit of sea rocket.

In Alabama

Perdido Key spans the state line. Critical habitat for the mouse includes a unit at Gulf State Park, along with Perdido Key State Park, Gulf Islands National Seashore, and private land on the Florida side.

Why it is at risk

Loss of dunes to building, feral cats, artificial lighting, and trash that draws predators and competitors. Moving mice between sites is credited with saving the population in the 1980s.

Notes

Critical habitat was revised by a rule published October 12, 2006 (71 FR 60238).
