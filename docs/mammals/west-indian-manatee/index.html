All mammals

West Indian manatee

Trichechus manatus

A large, slow, plant-eating mammal that summers in Alabama's coastal waters and winters in Florida.

What it looks like

A large aquatic mammal that never leaves the water. It grazes on seagrass and other water plants, and favors shallow grass beds near deeper channels.

Where it lives

Warm, shallow coastal waters, bays, and rivers with seagrass and a source of fresh water. In winter it must find warm water, such as springs and the outflows of power plants, because long exposure to cold can kill it.

In Alabama

A warm-season visitor. Manatees arrive on the northern Gulf coast around early April, as the water warms, and return to Florida by October. The Dauphin Island Sea Lab has tagged and tracked manatees in Alabama waters since 2009, and collects sightings from the public.

Why it is at risk

Collisions with boats, which are the leading cause of death from human activity, and the loss of warm-water refuges in winter. If you see one, slow down, cut the motor, and stay at least 100 feet away.

Notes

Listed as endangered in 1967, and reclassified as threatened by a rule published April 5, 2017 (82 FR 16668).
