Alabama beach mouse

Peromyscus polionotus ammobates

A small, pale mouse found only in the dunes of the Baldwin County coast.

What it looks like

A small, pale mouse of the dunes. Its light coat is an inherited trait.

Where it lives

Coastal dunes: the frontal dunes, and the higher scrub dunes behind them, which give refuge when storms wash over the beach.

How it lives

It is active at night and eats seeds, fruit, and insects. It breeds all year, most in winter, and an average mouse lives about nine months.

In Alabama

Found only in Baldwin County, on the Fort Morgan Peninsula and in Gulf Shores and Orange Beach, including the Perdue Unit of Bon Secour National Wildlife Refuge. The Service's review put its range at about 2,450 acres along some 13 miles of coast.

Why it is at risk

Loss of dunes to building, hurricanes, feral cats, artificial lighting, and a rising sea. The mouse recovered slowly from the hurricanes of 2004 and 2005, and its numbers swing widely.
