Florida panther

Puma concolor coryi

The big cat of the Southeast, probably the cougar that once ranged over Alabama, and gone from the state since the 1800s.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This mammal is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mammal. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A large, long-tailed cat, pale brown or rusty above and whitish or buff below, with dark marks on the tail tip, the backs of the ears, and the sides of the nose. Males weigh about 100 to 150 pounds and measure nearly 7 feet from nose to tail tip. Females are smaller.

Where it lives

In Alabama, cougars kept to remote uplands, rough country, and bottomland swamps.

In Alabama

Cougars once lived across Alabama, and the Florida panther was probably the kind found over much of the state. They likely disappeared around the middle of the 1800s. Sightings reported today usually prove to be bobcats, coyotes, house pets, or escaped captives.

Why it is at risk

The profile read for this entry does not list threats. The recovery plan covers them.
