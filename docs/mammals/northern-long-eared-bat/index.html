Northern long-eared bat

Myotis septentrionalis

A long-eared forest bat that white-nose syndrome has all but emptied from its wintering caves.

What it looks like

A small bat, 3 to 3.7 inches long, with a brown back, a paler belly, and notably long ears.

Where it lives

It winters in cold, humid caves and mines where the air is still. In the warm months it roosts under bark and in tree cavities, mostly in forest.

How it lives

Females gather in maternity colonies, often of 30 to 60 bats, and each bears one pup.

In Alabama

Its range in the state is poorly known. Small numbers have been recorded in the Bear Creek system and Bankhead National Forest in the northwest, and it likely lives in other forested uplands across north Alabama. The state has also reported a breeding population on the coast.

Why it is at risk

White-nose syndrome above all: counts at affected wintering sites have fallen by 97 to 100 percent. Wind turbines, loss of summer habitat, and disturbance in winter add to it.

Notes

Listed as threatened in 2015, and reclassified as endangered by a rule published November 30, 2022 (87 FR 73488).
