Indiana bat

Myotis sodalis

A small bat that winters packed together in a few cold caves and summers under the loose bark of trees.

What it looks like

A medium-sized bat much like the common little brown bat, with dull grayish-chestnut fur, pinkish-cinnamon underparts, and small, delicate hind feet.

Where it lives

It winters in cool caves and abandoned mines where the temperature holds steady. In summer it roosts under the peeling bark of large trees, often dead ones, in forest.

How it lives

Indiana bats hibernate in tight clusters on cave ceilings. Females gather in maternity colonies and usually raise one pup a year.

In Alabama

Found only in the northern third of the state. Nine cave systems have records, though some may rest on misidentified bats. Sauta Cave held the largest wintering group, about 250 bats from 1992 to 2002, and Bankhead National Forest holds two more sites. No maternity colony is known in Alabama.

Why it is at risk

Disturbance of hibernating bats, the commercial use of caves, loss of summer roost trees, pesticides, and white-nose syndrome, a fungal disease. The population has roughly halved since it was listed.
