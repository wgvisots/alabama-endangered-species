Tricolored bat

Perimyotis subflavus

One of the smallest bats in North America, once common across Alabama and now proposed for listing as endangered.

Proposed, not listed. This mammal has no protection under the Endangered Species Act unless a final rule lists it.

What it looks like

About 3 to 3.5 inches long. Each hair is dark at the base, pale in the middle, and dark at the tip. The coat often looks yellowish, but can be gray, brown, or black.

Where it lives

From spring to fall it roosts among the leaves of hardwood trees. In winter it hibernates in caves and mines, and in the South also in culverts and trees.

How it lives

It usually roosts alone, and feeds on small insects over water and along forest edges. Females usually bear two young.

In Alabama

Found statewide, and long counted among the most common bats in Alabama. In winter almost any sizable cave is likely to hold some. In the south of the state it may feed all year in warm weather.

Why it is at risk

White-nose syndrome has cut winter colonies by 90 to 100 percent at affected sites.

Notes

The Service proposed listing the bat as endangered in September 2022. It has no row on the federal list as of the date this site was checked.
