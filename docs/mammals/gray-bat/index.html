Gray bat

Myotis grisescens

A cave bat that spends the whole year underground, with most of its kind wintering in a handful of caves.

What it looks like

A medium-sized bat about 3.5 inches long, with a wingspan of 10 to 11 inches. Its fur is gray, and often fades to reddish-brown by early summer.

Where it lives

Caves, all year, in limestone country. Summer colonies sometimes use dams, mines, culverts, and the undersides of bridges. It feeds over open water.

How it lives

Gray bats roost on cave ceilings and are easily disturbed. About 95 percent of them hibernate in nine caves, which leaves the species exposed to a single bad event.

In Alabama

Found across the state except the southwest quarter. It is common near the Tennessee River in north Alabama, with smaller numbers in the central and southern parts. Fern Cave, in north Alabama, is one of the nine great wintering caves.

Why it is at risk

Disturbance of roosting bats by people, vandalism, the sealing or poor gating of caves, pesticides, and floods. Wind turbines and a changing climate are newer concerns.

Notes

Most of the major wintering caves are now permanently protected.
