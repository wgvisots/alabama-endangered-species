All mussels

Southern kidneyshell

Ptychobranchus jonesi

A mussel lost from two of its three river systems, and left only in the Choctawhatchee.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

An elliptical shell, up to about 2.8 inches long, smooth and shiny, greenish-yellow to dark brown or black, sometimes faintly rayed, and bluish white inside.

Where it lives

Streams with at least some current.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Escambia, Yellow, and Choctawhatchee river systems. It is now found only in the Choctawhatchee, including the Pea River and the West Fork, in Barbour, Coffee, Dale, Geneva, and Pike Counties.

Why it is at risk

Silt, shifting stream beds, and polluted water, above all. Dams, gravel mining, dredging, and the clearing of snags add to the harm, and floods and droughts fall hard on populations this small.
