All mussels

Chipola slabshell

Elliptio chipolaensis

A chestnut, banded mussel of the Chipola River, last recorded in Alabama from one creek in Houston County.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This mussel is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A medium-sized shell, about 3.3 inches long, oval and somewhat swollen, chestnut, often with alternating light and dark bands, and salmon-colored inside.

Where it lives

Muddy sand in moderate current.

In Alabama

The state's mussel list counts it as gone from Alabama, and gives its last record as a creek in Houston County. The Alabama Natural Heritage Program's list of 2021 does not agree: it ranks the mussel as critically imperiled in the state, not as gone.

Why it is at risk

Dams, channel work, pollution, and silt took most of its habitat. Erosion from the land, the drawing off of water, and spills still threaten it, and the populations left are small, cut off, and raising few young.
