All mussels

Alabama hickorynut

Obovaria unicolor

A round, thick mussel of the Tombigbee country, common only in the Sipsey River, and proposed for listing as endangered.

Proposed, not listed. This mussel has no protection under the Endangered Species Act unless a final rule lists it.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel.

What it looks like

A round to oval shell, fairly thick and somewhat swollen, 2 to 2.8 inches long, usually white inside and now and then pink.

Where it lives

Large creeks and rivers with bottoms of sand, gravel, or silt, in slow to moderate current.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Alabama and Tombigbee river systems. It is gone from the Alabama and Cahaba Rivers. What remains is in the Tombigbee system, on about a quarter of its former range, in rivers that include the Buttahatchee, Sipsey, Luxapallila, Lubbub, Noxubee, and Sucarnoochee. The Sipsey River is its stronghold.

Why it is at risk

Silt, altered flows, pollution, development, and a changing climate.
