All mussels

Orangenacre mucket

Hamiota perovalis

A mussel with a rose-pink lining that packs its young into a lure a fish will strike.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel.

What it looks like

A medium-sized, oval, fairly thick shell, 2 to 3.5 inches long, lined with rose or pink.

Where it lives

Clean streams and small rivers, in stable sand, gravel, and cobble, in moderate to swift current.

How it lives

It is one of at least three mussels that bundle their larvae into a long, trailing packet that looks like a small fish. A larger fish that strikes at it takes the larvae onto its gills. This was discovered in 1993.

In Alabama

The recovery plan of 2000 knew it from the Sipsey River and the tributaries of the Sipsey Fork, in Winston, Lawrence, Pickens, and Greene Counties; from the North River in Tuscaloosa and Fayette Counties; from the Locust and Blackburn Forks in Blount County; and from the Cahaba and Little Cahaba Rivers in Bibb, Jefferson, and Shelby Counties.

Why it is at risk

Dams buried its shoals under still water and cut its populations off from one another. Dredging, channel work, and gravel mining destroyed more, and runoff from mines, towns, and farms fouls what is left.
