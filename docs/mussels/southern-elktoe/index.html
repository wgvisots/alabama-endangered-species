All mussels

Southern elktoe

Alasmidonta triangulata

A thin, somewhat triangular mussel of the Chattahoochee country, proposed for listing as endangered.

Proposed, not listed. This mussel has no protection under the Endangered Species Act unless a final rule lists it.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel.

What it looks like

A medium-sized, fairly thin, somewhat triangular shell, up to about 2.8 inches long. Adults are olive brown to black. Young ones are yellowish brown to olive, often with dark green rays.

Where it lives

Creeks and rivers that flow all year, in the slow water near the banks, in silty mud, sand, and gravel. It does not gather in dense beds.

In Alabama

The state's list calls it imperiled, and last reported it from two creeks of the Chattahoochee system. The Service's proposal counts one site on the middle Chattahoochee River since 2000, and its proposed critical habitat takes in part of Russell County.

Why it is at risk

The drawing off of water for farms, runoff carrying silt and pollutants, the growth of towns, dams, and navigation channels.
