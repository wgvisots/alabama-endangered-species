All mussels

Oval pigtoe

Pleurobema pyriforme

A small, shiny, rayless mussel that may hang on in one creek in Houston County.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A small to medium shell, about 2.4 inches long, flattened and somewhat egg-shaped, shiny, yellowish to dark brown, without rays, and marked with distinct growth lines.

Where it lives

The sources read for this entry do not describe its habitat.

In Alabama

It was first described from the Chattahoochee River in Russell County, and once lived in several creeks there and in Houston County. The recovery plan of 2003 had a record of 2002 from one creek in Houston County. The Encyclopedia of Alabama counts it among the listed animals no longer found in the state.

Why it is at risk

Dams, channel work, pollution, and silt took most of its habitat. Erosion from the land, the drawing off of water, and spills still threaten it, and the populations left are small, cut off, and raising few young.
