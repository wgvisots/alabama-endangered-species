Hamiota subangulata

A glossy yellow-brown mussel with emerald rays, left in two creeks on the eastern edge of Alabama.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel.

What it looks like

A medium-sized, shiny shell, about 3.3 inches long, light yellowish-brown with bright emerald-green rays. Old ones can be much darker, with the rays faint.

Where it lives

Now mostly small tributary streams.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Chattahoochee River and in creeks of Russell, Barbour, and Houston Counties. Two groups remain in Alabama: one in a creek in Russell County, and one in a creek in Houston County.

Why it is at risk

Dams, channel work, pollution, and silt took most of its habitat. Erosion from the land, the drawing off of water, and spills still threaten it, and the populations left are small, cut off, and raising few young.
