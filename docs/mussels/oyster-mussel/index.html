All mussels

Oyster mussel

Epioblasma capsaeformis

A mussel that once reached Muscle Shoals and the Paint Rock and Elk Rivers, now being returned below Wilson Dam.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This mussel is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel.

Where it lives

Small to medium rivers, and sometimes large ones, on bottoms of coarse sand to boulders, in moderate to swift current.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Tennessee River down to Muscle Shoals, and in the Paint Rock and Elk Rivers. The state counts it as gone, and reports that it is being reintroduced in the tailwaters of Wilson Dam.

Why it is at risk

Chiefly the dams. The shoals of the Tennessee River, where it lived, now lie under reservoirs, and what is left of its range is broken into pieces. Silt and pollution press on the survivors.

Notes

A rule of June 14, 2001 (66 FR 32250) also set up an experimental population of this mussel in the free-flowing reach of the Tennessee River below Wilson Dam, in Colbert and Lauderdale Counties.
