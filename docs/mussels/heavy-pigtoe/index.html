All mussels

Heavy pigtoe

Pleurobema taitianum

A thick, triangular mussel down to a single known population, of about 80 animals, in the Alabama River.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A thick, swollen, triangular shell, up to about 2.8 inches long, brown, olive brown, or black, usually without rays.

Where it lives

Rivers with bottoms of gravel and coarse sand, often in deep water.

In Alabama

It once lived throughout the Tombigbee and Alabama Rivers and the lower reaches of their larger tributaries. The only population known today is in a short reach of the Alabama River in Dallas County. It was put at 81 animals in 2011, and no young have been found.

Why it is at risk

The Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway destroyed most of its habitat. Dams, dredging, the clearing of snags, silt, and reduced flow have done the rest.
