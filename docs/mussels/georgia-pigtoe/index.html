All mussels

Georgia pigtoe

Pleurobema hanleyianum

A mussel that once lived along more than 300 miles of the Coosa system, and survives at a few shoals of one river outside Alabama.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This mussel is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

An oval to elliptical, somewhat swollen shell, 2 to 2.5 inches long, yellowish-tan to reddish-brown, sometimes ringed with green, and white to dull bluish-white inside.

Where it lives

Shallow runs and riffles in strong to moderate current, over coarse sand, gravel, and cobble.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Coosa River and its tributaries in Alabama. No Alabama population is known today.

Why it is at risk

Dams, which broke its range into pieces that cannot reach one another, along with runoff carrying silt and fertilizer, spills, drought, and the small size of what remains.
