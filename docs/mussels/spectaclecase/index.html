All mussels

Spectaclecase

Cumberlandia monodonta

A long, sometimes curved mussel, up to nine inches, that shelters under slabs of rock in big rivers.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel. The Fish & Wildlife Service profile is the best place to look for one.

What it looks like

A large, greatly lengthened shell, sometimes curved, reaching at least 9 inches. Young shells are light yellow, greenish-tan, or brown. Old ones are rough and dark brown to black. The inside is white.

Where it lives

Large rivers, in riffles and shoals, often sheltered under slabs of rock or ledges.

In Alabama

The Muscle Shoals reach of the Tennessee River once held a large population, and dams took most of its habitat. The listing rule knew of about ten live mussels found at three sites below Guntersville Dam between 1997 and 1999, and called the status of the Tennessee River population unknown.

Why it is at risk

Dams and reservoirs, which flooded its habitat and split what was left, and the cold water let out below them. Zebra mussels are a further threat, and few young are being raised.
