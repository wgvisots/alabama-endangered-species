All mussels

Triangular kidneyshell

Ptychobranchus greenii

A flattened, straw-yellow mussel of the shoals of the upper Mobile basin.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel.

What it looks like

An oval to elliptical, flattened shell, up to about 4 inches long, straw-yellow to yellow-brown.

Where it lives

Shoals and runs of sand, gravel, and cobble in small rivers and large streams.

In Alabama

It lives above the Fall Line. The recovery plan of 2000 knew it from the Sipsey Fork in Winston and Lawrence Counties, the Locust Fork in Blount County, and Coosa tributaries in Shelby and Cherokee Counties. Only one recently dead shell was known from the Cahaba.

Why it is at risk

Dams buried its shoals under still water and cut its populations off from one another. Dredging, channel work, and gravel mining destroyed more, and runoff from mines, towns, and farms fouls what is left.
