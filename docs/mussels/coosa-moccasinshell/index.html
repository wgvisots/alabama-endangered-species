All mussels

Coosa moccasinshell

Medionidus parvulus

A small, thin mussel of the Coosa and Cahaba that now survives only in the headwaters, in Georgia and Tennessee.

Thought to be gone from Alabama. This mussel is on the federal list and once lived in the state, but it is not known here today.

No photo here yet. This site only shows photos that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and none has been found for this mussel.

What it looks like

A small, thin, long shell, seldom much over an inch and a half, yellow-brown to dark brown with fine green rays, and bluish inside.

Where it lives

Shoals of sand, gravel, and cobble in small rivers and large streams, in moderate to strong current.

In Alabama

It once lived in the Cahaba River, the Sipsey Fork, and the tributaries of the Coosa. No Alabama population is known today.

Why it is at risk

Dams buried its shoals under still water and cut its populations off from one another. Dredging, channel work, and gravel mining destroyed more, and runoff from mines, towns, and farms fouls what is left.
